Invited Speakers

Nine perspectives on bringing safe robots into real-world use.

Researchers, practitioners and safety specialists connect research, deployment and validation across three application domains.

Topics below follow the workshop proposal. Final talk titles and abstracts will be added as the program is finalised.

Healthcare, Assistive, and Surgical Robotics

Academic perspective

Arianna Menciassi

Scuola Superiore Sant’Anna, Italy

Topic: Surgical robot development

Arianna Menciassi is Professor of Biomedical Robotics at Scuola Superiore Sant’Anna and leads the Surgical Robotics and Allied Technologies area at The BioRobotics Institute. Her research spans surgical robotics, biomedical microrobotics, and smart and soft medical devices.

Profile and research · Photo source

Practitioner perspective

Dirk Wilhelm

Technical University of Munich / TUM University Hospital, Germany

Topic: Practical surgical robotics

Dirk Wilhelm is Professor of Medical Robotics at the Technical University of Munich and a practising surgeon and senior physician at TUM University Hospital. He leads the MITI research group, working on minimally invasive and robot-assisted surgery and the integration of robotic assistance into clinical workflows.

Profile and research · Photo source

Certification & validation perspective

Tamás Haidegger

Óbuda University, Hungary

Topic: Safety and certification of surgical robots

Tamás Haidegger is a professor at Óbuda University and leads medical robotics research at its Antal Bejczy Center for Intelligent Robotics. His work includes surgical robot control and teleoperation, image-guided therapy, and medical technologies. He contributes to international standardisation on the safety and performance of medical robots.

Industry and Manufacturing

Academic perspective

Nadia Figueroa

University of Pennsylvania, USA

Topic: Safe adaptive control

Nadia Figueroa is the Shalini and Rajeev Misra Presidential Assistant Professor in Mechanical Engineering and Applied Mechanics at the University of Pennsylvania and a member of GRASP. Her research combines learning, control and perception to enable robots to adapt to people and changing environments, with an emphasis on safe physical human–robot collaboration.

Practitioner perspective

Seungju Lee

Korea Electronics Technology Institute (KETI), Republic of Korea

Topic: Industrial robot deployment

Seungju Lee heads the Spatial Intelligence Research Center at the Korea Electronics Technology Institute (KETI). The centre develops LiDAR, sensor fusion, spatial perception and AI-based 3D object recognition. His work includes LiDAR development and commercialisation, bringing an applied sensing and industrial technology perspective to the workshop.

Certification & validation perspective

Benedikt Pulver

TÜV SÜD, Germany

Topic: Safety and certification of industrial machinery

Benedikt Pulver heads the Machine Safety department at TÜV SÜD Product Service. His work covers testing and certification of machinery, including robots, automated guided vehicles and mobile platforms. His experience spans mechanical, electrical and functional safety, as well as the assessment of emerging automation technologies.

Beyond Structured Workspaces: Field and High-Consequence Robotics

Academic perspective

Tetsuya Kimura

Nagaoka University of Technology, Japan

Topic: Field safety research

Tetsuya Kimura is a professor at Nagaoka University of Technology working in system safety and rescue robotics. His research connects robot development with risk assessment, practical deployment and international safety standards, with a particular focus on disaster-response and rescue robots.

Profile and research · Photo source

Practitioner perspective

Ipek Caliskanelli

UK Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA), UK

Topic: Nuclear and fusion robotics applications

Ipek Caliskanelli is a Principal Robotics Research Engineer at the UK Atomic Energy Authority. Her work focuses on interoperability, coordination and collaboration among robotic systems in high-consequence environments, and on bringing robotics research into nuclear applications. She is also an Honorary Visiting Lecturer in Computer Science at the University of York.

Certification & validation session contributor

Michela Rossi

European Commission, Joint Research Centre (JRC)

Topic: Robotics for maintenance

Michela Rossi is a building engineer and researcher at the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre. Her research includes structural health monitoring and the assessment of existing structures. Her work on construction and building-renovation robotics brings a built-environment perspective to the workshop’s discussion of maintenance, deployment and safety validation.
